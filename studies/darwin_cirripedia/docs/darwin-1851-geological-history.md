## Inferential Calculations & Methodological Formalizations

This section articulates the exact arithmetic operations Darwin runs across these blocks,

### 1. The Global Calibrating Ratio (Modern Ocean Basin vs. Cretaceous Quarry)

<!-- id: :calc.darwin-1851/global-calibrating-ratio -->

**Associated Verbatim Passages:** `:cr.darwin-1851/culminant-point`, `:cr.darwin-1851/modern-ocean-baseline`

Darwin evaluates the biological significance of local species richness by constructing
a comparative ratio against global modern oceans rather than relying on an abstract
scaling assumption.

**Empirical Vector:**
- Modern attached Pedunculata by geographic province:
  $$\mathbf{S}_{\text{recent}} = \{ \text{Mediterranean}: 3, \, \text{New Zealand}: 3, \, \text{Australia}: 3, \, \text{Madeira}: 4, \, \text{Philippines}: 5 \}$$
  $$\text{Mean modern province richness: } \bar{S}_{\text{recent}} = 3.6 \text{ species}$$

- Cretaceous single-quarry richness:
  $$S_{\text{Norwich}} \in [9, 12] \text{ species (Upper Chalk)}$$
  $$S_{\text{Scania}} \in [9, 10] \text{ species (Maestricht stage)}$$

**The Calibrating Ratio:**
$$\mathcal{R}_{\text{culminant}} = \frac{S_{\text{fossil, single locality}}}{\bar{S}_{\text{recent, whole ocean basin}}} \approx \frac{9.5}{3.6} \approx 2.6$$

**Inferential Result:**
A single Upper Chalk quarry in Norfolk sustained $\sim 2.6 \times$ the stalked barnacle
diversity of an entire living ocean basin (e.g., Mediterranean or Australian shelf).
This ratio empirically warrants Darwin's claim that the Cretaceous represents the
"culminant point" of Lepadidae without requiring an unobserved global census.

### 2. The Fitch Inverse Ratio: Decoupling Richness from Biomass

<!-- id: :calc.darwin-1851/fitch-inverse-ratio -->

**Associated Verbatim Passages:** `:cr.darwin-1851/fitch-inverse-ratio`, `:cr.darwin-1851/hooker-no-relation`

Darwin establishes an explicit negative empirical rule: **high taxonomic diversification
does not imply high standing individual density or biomass.**

**The Physical Inventory of the Fitch Drawer:**
- Locality: Upper Chalk pits near Norwich, Norfolk, England
- Sampling Effort ($\Delta t_{\text{effort}}$): 20 years continuous collecting by Robert Fitch
- Verified diagnostic valves recovered across all 20 years:
  - *Scalpellum maximum*: 9 carinae
  - *Scalpellum fossula*: 6 carinae
  - *Pollicipes striatus*: 2 scuta
  - *Pollicipes fallax*: 2 scuta
  - *Pollicipes angelini*: 4 scuta
  - **Total verified diagnostic specimens:** $N = 23$ valves across $S = 5$ species.

**The Sampling Rate:**
$$\text{Yield Rate} = \frac{N}{\Delta t_{\text{effort}}} = \frac{23 \text{ valves}}{20 \text{ years}} = 1.15 \text{ valves/year}$$

**The Decoupling Invariant (Hooker-Darwin Rule):**
Let $S$ be standing species richness and $B$ be standing biomass (proportional to specimen density $N/V$).
The naive ecological prior assumes positive monotonicity:
$$\frac{\partial B}{\partial S} > 0 \quad (\text{rejected})$$

Darwin demonstrates that in the rock:
$$\text{Cov}(S, B) \approx 0$$

A depositional environment can record an intense morphological radiation ($S = 5 \text{ to } 12$)
under physical conditions where standing individual populations are vanishingly rare
($1.15 \text{ identifiable valves/year}$).

### 3. The Discovery Rate as an Index of Unrecorded Vastness

<!-- id: :calc.darwin-1851/discovery-rate-index -->

**Associated Verbatim Passage:** `:cr.darwin-1851/sparseness-as-index`

Darwin uses the marginal discovery rate across small private cabinets as an estimator of
the unrecorded scale of the Cretaceous ocean.

**Formulation:**
Let $C_1, C_2, \dots, C_k$ be independent small amateur cabinets examined.
Darwin observes that the marginal discovery rate $\frac{\Delta S_{\text{new}}}{\Delta C}$ does not saturate:
$$\frac{d S_{\text{observed}}}{d C} \approx \text{constant} > 0$$

Furthermore:
$$\frac{S_{\text{Fitch}}}{S_{\text{global, Upper Chalk}}} \approx \frac{5}{11} \approx 0.45$$

**Inferential Result:**
If a single collector ($k=1$) in one market town accounts for nearly half ($45\%$) of all
globally known species from that stage, the collector has not exhausted the biota;
rather, the global rock record is severely undersampled. The smallness of the drawer's
census operates as a direct index of the vastness of the uncollected marine reality.

### 4. The Horizontal-to-Vertical Range Invariant

<!-- id: :calc.darwin-1851/horizontal-vertical-invariant -->

**Associated Verbatim Passage:** `:cr.darwin-1851/vertical-range-law`

Darwin derives a formal continuity expectation: deep-time stratigraphic persistence
is coupled to spatial geographic range.

**Formulation:**
Let $A_{\text{geo}}(s)$ be the horizontal geographic range of species $s$, and
$T_{\text{strat}}(s)$ be the vertical duration (number of chronostratigraphic stages spanned).

Based on the known dispersal capacity of recent cirripede cyprid larvae:
$$P\left( T_{\text{strat}}(s) \ge 2 \right) \propto A_{\text{geo}}(s)$$

**Empirical Verification against Darwin's 1851 Master Table:**
- Species exhibiting confirmed wide geographic range (England, Denmark, Scania):
  - *Scalpellum maximum*: Spans Lower Chalk, Upper Chalk, and Maestricht ($T = 3$ stages).
  - *Pollicipes glaber*: Spans Lower Chalk, Upper Chalk, and Maestricht ($T = 3 \text{ to } 4$ stages).
- Species with restricted local occurrences: $T = 1$ stage (confined to single horizons).

The observation of multi-stage persistence confirms continuous biological descent
and unbroken deposition across chalk horizons, rather than catastrophic faunal re-creation.

### 5. Architectural Invariance under Complete Specific Turnover

<!-- id: :calc.darwin-1851/architectural-invariance -->

**Associated Verbatim Passage:** `:cr.darwin-1851/generic-persistence`

Darwin contrasts the complete turnover of terminal species with the multi-era stability of
the functional valve architecture.

**The Matrix:**
- Species-level survival across era boundaries:
  $$P(s \in \text{Oolite} \land s \in \text{Cretaceous}) = 0$$
  $$P(s \in \text{Cretaceous} \land s \in \text{Tertiary}) = 0$$
  $$P(s \in \text{Tertiary} \land s \in \text{Recent}) = 0$$
  $$\text{Species Turnover} = 100\%$$

- Genus-level persistence:
  $$\text{Span}(\text{Pollicipes}) \ge 10 \text{ stages (Lower Oolite } \to \text{ Recent, } > 150 \text{ Myr)}$$
  $$\text{Span}(\text{Scalpellum}) \ge 8 \text{ stages (Lower Greensand } \to \text{ Recent, } > 110 \text{ Myr)}$$

**Inferential Result:**
Macroevolutionary change operates on two completely decoupled timescales:
1. High-frequency terminal speciation ($100\%$ extinction and replacement per era).
2. Deep-time conservative valve homology (carina-scuta-terga geometric articulation
   persisting across $> 150\text{ Myr}$).
